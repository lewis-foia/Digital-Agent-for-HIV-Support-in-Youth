What is HIV?
HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a virus that attacks the immune system, weakening the body’s ability to fight infections and diseases.

What is AIDS?
AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) is the most advanced stage of HIV infection, where the immune system is badly damaged and the person becomes vulnerable to opportunistic infections.

How is HIV transmitted?
HIV can be transmitted through unprotected sexual contact, sharing contaminated needles, transfusion of infected blood, and from mother to child during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding.

Can HIV be transmitted through casual contact?
No, HIV is not spread through hugging, shaking hands, sharing dishes, or casual contact.

Is there a cure for HIV?
Currently, there is no cure for HIV, but effective treatment with antiretroviral therapy (ART) allows people to live long and healthy lives.

What is ART?
Antiretroviral therapy (ART) is a combination of medicines that slows the progression of HIV and helps prevent transmission to others.

Why is adherence to treatment important?
Adherence to ART is crucial. Taking medicines regularly helps keep the virus suppressed, protects the immune system, and prevents drug resistance.

What are common challenges for young people living with HIV?
They may face stigma, discrimination, mental health struggles, and difficulties adhering to treatment.

What diet is recommended for people living with HIV?
A balanced diet including proteins, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains helps maintain good health and supports the immune system.

Can HIV be prevented?
Yes. Using condoms, avoiding sharing needles, and taking preventive medications (like PrEP) significantly reduce the risk of HIV infection.
